What is a good CCAT score?

Short answer: the average score is about 24 out of 50. If you get more than 24 right, you did better than half the people who take it. Most competitive jobs want to see 30 or higher.

Your CCAT score is just the number of questions you answered correctly, out of 50. There's no partial credit and no penalty for guessing. What makes the test hard isn't the questions — it's that you only get 15 minutes, which is about 18 seconds per question. Fewer than 1% of people finish all 50.

What score do employers actually want?

There's no universal passing score. Each employer picks a cutoff for each role, so the same score can be great for one job and short for another. As a rough guide, the more analytical the role, the higher the bar:

  • Entry-level and support roles: often around the average, so low-to-mid 20s.
  • Sales roles: commonly mid-to-high 20s.
  • Analysts, engineers, and technical roles: usually 30 or above.
  • Management and executive roles: typically 30+, sometimes considerably higher.
  • Highly selective employers: some set the bar in the high 30s or above.

If a recruiter told you a target score, trust that over any chart. If they didn't, aiming for 30+ is a safe goal for most professional roles.

How CCAT scoring works

  • Raw score = questions correct. Nothing is subtracted for wrong answers.
  • Never leave a blank. Since wrong answers don't hurt you, a guess is strictly better than nothing.
  • You can't skip and come back. Once you move past a question, it's gone.
  • Questions get harder as you go. The early ones are the cheapest points — don't rush them into mistakes.

Can you improve your CCAT score?

Yes, and usually faster than people expect. The test measures how quickly you work through unfamiliar problems — and "unfamiliar" is the part you can fix. When you've seen the question types before, you stop losing seconds to figuring out what's being asked. Most of the improvement between a first and third practice test comes from pacing and recognition, not from getting smarter.

Related questions

  • Is there a passing score? No — employers set their own cutoffs.
  • Do I see my score? Usually not. The employer receives it, and most don't share it.
  • Can I retake it? Generally not for the same application. That's why practice beforehand matters.
