# Application Analysis — Creative Services Manager, A1 Garage Door Service

## Fit: Good. This is the best-calibrated creative leadership role you have seen.

The Flock Creative Director posting was a reach on level. This one is aimed at exactly where you sit, and the difference matters:

| | Flock Creative Director | A1 Creative Services Manager |
|---|---|---|
| Experience wanted | 12–15 years implied | **6–8 years. You have 7+** |
| Leadership bar | Managing discipline leads | **2+ years leading projects or developing people. You clear it** |
| PM platform | Not mentioned | **"Asana or a comparable platform." Wrike and Workamajig count** |
| Design systems | Core requirement | Listed, but weighted alongside campaigns and workflow |

## What you clear cleanly

- **The experience band.** Seven-plus years, right in the middle of 6–8. You are not stretching.
- **Team leadership and mentoring.** Six years directing teams of 5 to 20, plus two years developing 90+ creators a year with hands-on critique. Their bar is 2+ years. You have triple that.
- **Workflow ownership, which is a third of this job.** "Own the Creative Services workflow within Asana, including project intake, prioritization, capacity planning, timelines, approvals." You do this today in Wrike and Notion, and you have built the systems from nothing: an asset library that cut retrieval 50%, an approval process that halved revision rounds.
- **The creative asset library and brand standards.** Named in the posting, and you have real, measurable experience.
- **Performance reporting.** "Use campaign, email, website, and social metrics to recommend improvements" — you have a Master's in Marketing Analytics and numbers on the board: 20x engagement, 200% CTR, 100%+ paid and organic lift. Most creative managers cannot do this half of the job.
- **Multi-channel campaign work.** TV, paid social, organic, web. Genuine breadth.
- **Art direction across video and photography.** Yours.

## The gaps, named plainly

1. **Hands-on graphic design depth.** They want a portfolio showing "hands-on design execution" and a strong eye for "layout, typography, color, and visual hierarchy." You are a filmmaker with motion graphics and photo skills, not a designer. Color you own. **Typography is not in your record at all.**
2. **Design systems.** Same gap that appeared on the Flock Creative Director posting. You build *production* systems, which is adjacent but not the same as a design system.
3. **Print.** Listed under art direction and production. Nothing in your record.
4. **Email marketing platforms.** Still the standing gap. No Klaviyo, Mailchimp, or equivalent hands-on. Email is named repeatedly here.
5. **Asana specifically.** The posting says "or a comparable project management platform," so this is not disqualifying, but if you want it closed, [Asana Academy](https://academy.asana.com/) is free and takes an afternoon. Worth doing before an interview — it turns a caveat into a non-issue.

None of these is fatal on its own. Together they mean you should expect design-depth questions, and you should not oversell that side.

## What to check before applying

**Where is it?** The posting requires in-person collaboration 4–5 days a week and never says where. A1 was founded in Phoenix and is headquartered in the Valley, so this is probably local and an easy commute from Mesa — but confirm rather than assume. If it turns out to be a different market, everything changes.

**What is the pay?** Not listed. Creative Services Manager is a real management title with team and budget responsibility; ask for the band early rather than investing three interview rounds first. Your floor is $75k and this role should comfortably clear it, but get it in writing.

## How to position yourself

Lead with the combination almost no one else brings: **you manage, you make, and you measure.**

Most candidates for this role are designers who moved into management and cannot read a performance dashboard, or marketers who can read the dashboard but have never given art direction. You have directed crews, built the intake system, shot and cut the work, and hold a Master's in Marketing Analytics. That three-way overlap is your argument.

Second: **the operations half is underrated and you are strong there.** "Improve creative workflows, file management, quality assurance, and project delivery" is a line most applicants skim past. You have numbers on exactly that. In a fast-growing company drowning in requests, the person who fixes intake is worth more than the person with the prettiest portfolio.

## Interview talking points

- **The workflow story.** Walk them through how you built the Frame.io library and the storyboard approval process, and what each one saved. This maps directly onto the Asana ownership they describe.
- **Developing people.** 90+ students a year, 80+ beginners to 95% proficiency. That is real evidence you can grow a junior designer, which is what "manage and mentor" actually means day to day.
- **Be honest on design depth.** "My hands-on craft is strongest in video, motion, and photo direction. I direct and review graphic design rather than being the primary designer." That framing is accurate and appropriate for a manager role. Claiming to be a senior designer will collapse at the portfolio review.
- **Ask what the team looks like.** How many designers, what is in flight, where is the backlog worst. It signals you are thinking about the actual job.

## Bottom line

Apply, and rank it near the top alongside the Flock Senior Video Editor role. The experience band fits, the leadership bar is cleared, the workflow ownership is a genuine strength, and the performance-analytics half is a differentiator.

Confirm the location and get the pay band early. Do the free Asana course before an interview. And be straight about where your hands-on craft actually lives.
