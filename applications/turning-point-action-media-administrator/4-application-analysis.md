# Application Analysis — Media Administrator, Turning Point Action

## Fit: Strong

Of the two Turning Point Action postings you sent, **this is the one to pursue.** The Ballot Chaser role asked you to do work you have never done. This one asks you to do exactly what you have done for seven years, and it treats your Arizona Legislature experience as a bonus rather than a stretch.

Where you clear the bar:

- **"The ideal candidate would have long form editing and social media experience."** That sentence is your resume. Long-form documentaries and interviews at Lightbox, 200+ videos at Grand Canyon Education, and social initiatives at Charter One with numbers attached.
- **Short-form for social.** 20x engagement on one initiative, 200% click-through lift on another. Most editors applying here will show a reel and no numbers.
- **YouTube long-form.** You have reached over a million people on your own channel. For a role that names YouTube and Rumble as delivery targets, that is direct, verifiable evidence you understand the platform.
- **Track engagement and insights.** A Master's in Marketing with an analytics specialization backs this up beyond "I check the dashboard."
- **Deliver assets to marketing channels.** Frame.io and Dropbox, with a measured 50% cut in retrieval time. This is the "administrator" half of the title and you have it.
- **Fast-paced and organized.** 98% on time, 92% on budget.
- **"WOW" skill — political organizations.** Partial credit, and honest partial credit: the Arizona Legislature is government, not a campaign. But it is real political-environment work with recognizable names attached.

## Gaps — name them, do not paper over them

1. **No podcast editing.** They name podcast platforms twice. This is the second posting this week to want it. The honest bridge is the same: a podcast is a locked multicam interview with sustained audio, and you have cut cinematic interviews with proper lighting and audio for years. Say that. Do not claim podcast credits.
2. **No TikTok.** They list it by name alongside Instagram and X. You have Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube documented. TikTok is a platform, not a skill — the editing transfers — but do not put it on the resume as experience you have.
3. **No Rumble.** Same reasoning as TikTok. It is a delivery destination, not a craft.
4. **"Strong and efficient writing skills."** You script and storyboard, which is real, but you are not a documented copywriter. If they test writing, expect it.
5. **No prior Turning Point Action involvement.** That is the second "WOW" skill and you simply do not have it.

## The one thing only you can answer

The posting lists **"passion for conservative ideas and principles"** as a minimum qualification. As with the Ballot Chaser role, I left that claim out of your cover letter — a conviction is not a credential, and I will not write one on your behalf.

If it is true, say it in your own words and tell me; I will work it into the letter. It matters more here than at most employers, because the job is producing the messaging itself, not just cutting footage.

## Things to check before you apply

**The pay is not listed.** Again. And this one deserves a closer look than usual: the responsibilities describe an editor who owns long-form, short-form, analytics, and delivery, while the title "Media Administrator" reads like a coordinator role. Those two things often come with very different salary bands. Ask what the band is and who the role reports to before you get far in.

**In-office at Phoenix HQ.** From Mesa that is a normal Valley commute, similar to the Scottsdale role you queued this morning. Fine, but it is five days on site.

**Same visibility consideration as the Ballot Chaser role.** Producing content for a named political organization attaches your name to it publicly, in a way corporate media work does not. Some future employers read that as neutral, some do not. Worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering later.
