Company research

HH Global is a creative production and marketing procurement company headquartered in Leatherhead, UK. It bought the Chicago-based InnerWorkings in 2020 for $177m after Blackstone made a £100m minority investment in 2019. Profiles put its size at 4,000 to 4,500 staff in 55 to 64 countries, with 26 creative studios. Much of its work is placing in-house teams inside client companies. Several Glassdoor reviews come from people who say they worked as vendor or contract staff on Google, so a Google-account role would likely work the same way: HH Global employs you, and you do the work for Google. The CEO changed in October 2026. Mike Perez came back as group CEO, replacing Kristian Elgey, and Nadia Pelekanos was made deputy group CEO. Most review data is company-wide. It mixes warehouse, production, account, India-based and UK roles. Only a handful of reviews describe the Google account, and none of the ones the searches returned is clearly from a creative operations role.

As of September 2026, work-life balance was rated 3.3 out of 5 and had dropped 7% over the past year. Other sub-scores across snapshots: culture and values 3.0 to 3.3, career opportunities 2.8 to 3.1. Several reviews describe Google-account work. A marketing manager who was a contractor for Google praised the pay and full health benefits but said contract staff are treated as less than team members, with limited growth and leftover work. Another reviewer said moving from contractor to vendor status removed a time limit on their employment and came with benefits, but that raises only loosely track inflation and bonuses are not guaranteed because of Google's 'financial status.' What employees say: pros and cons

Pros

  • Reviewers often praise remote and hybrid flexibility, and the searches found no return-to-office mandate. HH Global posts remote US creative roles. [1] [2]
  • Colleagues and immediate teams get positive mentions on both Glassdoor and Indeed (for example, 'great team to work with' and 'teammates are very supportive'). [1] [2]
  • On the Google account, reviewers report solid pay and full health benefits. One said vendor status through HH Global removed the time limit that came with contractor status and offered benefits they can choose each year. [1] [2]
  • There is room to move between client accounts. One Indeed reviewer cited 'plenty of opportunity to move between accounts,' and a Glassdoor account director mentioned 'loads of opportunities to grow,' though also red tape. [1] [2]
  • It is a large company with financial backing: 4,000+ staff, 26 creative studios, a Blackstone minority investment, and the InnerWorkings acquisition. It is also putting money into creative work, including a new leadership team for its in-house agency Nuevo in July 2025 and postings for an in-house creative team serving a large tech client. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Cons

  • There are pay and bonus complaints. Reviewers say raises loosely track inflation, bonuses are mentioned every year but not paid even when targets are hit, and pay is lower than client full-time staff. AmbitionBox reviews (India-heavy) also cite low raises. [1] [2] [3]
  • Vendor staff on Google sit below Google employees. Reviewers say contract and vendor staff are treated as less than team members, get leftover work and limited growth, and receive yearly reviews through HH Global rather than Google, so feedback can be filtered. [1] [2] [3]
  • Job security is a concern. Glassdoor has a 'Lots of Layoffs' review and a page of layoff-tagged reviews, and one reviewer says Google can cut whole vendor teams without notice. An Indeed production reviewer also cited job insecurity. The searches found no official layoff announcement. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Management is the most common complaint. Reviewers describe a senior leadership team cut off from day-to-day work, micromanagement, poor communication between teams, and roles that were not defined. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Leadership has turned over several times. Perez was group CEO from 2021 to 2023 and Elgey led after that. In October 2026 Perez returned and replaced Elgey, and a deputy group CEO was named at the same time. You would join during a fresh transition. [1] [2] [3]
  • Ratings are mediocre and falling. Overall Glassdoor scores are 3.2 to 3.3, only 51 to 61% would recommend, career opportunities score 2.8 to 3.1, and work-life balance fell 7% in the year to September 2026. Some account and production reviewers report long hours and stressful workloads. [1] [2] [3]

Watch for

  • The CEO changed in October 2026 (Perez back in, Elgey out, new deputy CEO). New leadership often brings restructuring. Ask how the change affects the Google account.
  • Reviews tagged with layoffs exist, including one saying a layoff came with no explanation and HR knew two days ahead. Another review says Google can drop whole vendor teams without notice. The role's security depends on the Google contract.
  • Bonuses are described as promised every year but not paid, and as 'not guaranteed' because of Google's finances. Get any bonus terms in writing.
  • Vendor staff say they get second-class treatment on the Google account: leftover work, limited growth, and reviews filtered through HH Global.
  • Several Glassdoor review titles are harsh ('most toxic corporate culture,' 'horrible company, unethical,' 'avoid'). Dates vary and some are old.
  • A July 2024 headline said Blackstone was weighing a sale of its stake. It is unverified, but an ownership change is possible.
  • The sample is thin. Few reviews are specific to Google or creative work, and the searches returned no Reddit threads.

Bottom line

HH Global looks like a middling employer (about 3.2 to 3.4 out of 5 across Glassdoor and Indeed), and the Google-account roles have known trade-offs. The good: remote flexibility, decent benefits, supportive teammates, and the chance to work on Google projects. The bad: you would be vendor staff, not a Google employee, with reported limits on growth, bonuses that may not be paid, raises close to inflation, and job security tied to Google's contract. Add recurring management complaints and an October 2026 CEO change. The evidence is company-wide, with only a handful of Google-account reviews and none clearly from creative operations, so treat it as a rough signal. In interviews, ask about the length and renewal of the Google contract, bonus terms in writing, how performance reviews work between HH Global and Google, the growth path, and whether the CEO change will mean restructuring.
