# Required Application Response — Favorite AI Tool / Prompt Workflow

Vici asks for this alongside the resume, max two paragraphs. This is the strongest card in the application, so it is worth submitting exactly as written rather than improvising in the form field.

**Everything below is true to your actual work. Nothing invented.**

---

## Paste this

> My most valuable workflow is Claude with agentic coding, used as a production system rather than a chat window. At Charter One I built a workflow where Claude handles the repeatable layers of content production — drafting scripts and variants from a brief, restructuring copy for each channel, and generating the supporting documentation a campaign needs — while I keep the judgment calls and the storytelling. It cut our production times in half. The part that made it work was treating prompts as reusable assets: I keep tested prompt structures for each recurring job, so the output is consistent instead of a new experiment every time. That consistency is what turns AI from a novelty into throughput.
>
> I use the same approach across the wider stack. Running my own company, I cut creative production time by 30% by putting AI at specific points in the pipeline — ChatGPT and Claude for structure and copy, ElevenLabs for voice, Runway and the built-in Adobe and DaVinci tools for the production steps that used to eat hours. My rule is to automate the repeatable work and protect the creative decisions, because the tools accelerate execution but they do not know the audience. The result is more campaigns shipped per week with the quality holding, which is the only measure that matters.

---

## Why this answer works

- **It names a specific tool and a specific outcome** — half the production time — rather than listing tools.
- **"Prompts as reusable assets"** shows systems thinking, which is what they are actually screening for in a role that owns operational workflow.
- **The second paragraph proves range** without turning into a tool inventory.
- **"Automate the repeatable work and protect the creative decisions"** is a point of view. Reviewers remember a point of view; they forget a list.
- It stays in your register: plain, direct, no hype.

## If the field allows more

Offer to walk them through it live: *"Happy to screen-share the actual workflow — it is easier to show than describe."* For an AI-forward team, that offer often converts better than the answer itself.
