# Outreach — HVAC Service Technician

LinkedIn matters less in the trades than in media. HVAC shops hire through phone calls, walk-ins, referrals, and Indeed. Adjust accordingly.

**The employer is not named in the posting you sent.** Get the company name from the listing first — none of this works without it.

## What actually works in this industry

1. **Call the shop.** Ask for the Service Manager by name. Trades hiring happens on the phone far more than through portals, and a career changer who calls stands out from a stack of identical applications.
2. **Walk in.** Shops open early. Showing up at 7am in work clothes, asking for five minutes, tells them more about you than any resume line.
3. **LinkedIn** for the Service Manager, Operations Manager, or owner if the company has a presence. Most small Valley shops do not.

## Phone or in-person script

> Hi, I'm Joshua Chappell. I'm calling about the service technician posting. I'll be upfront: I don't have three years of HVAC yet. I ran my own field service business for seven years, held 5.0-star reviews for over a year, and I've spent years working on construction and industrial job sites, so attics and roofs are normal for me. I'm working on my EPA 608. If the service role needs more experience, do you have anything on the install side I could start on?

That last question is the whole point of the call. It converts a "no" into the job you can actually get.

## If you find the Service Manager on LinkedIn

> Hi [Name] — I applied for your HVAC Service Technician opening. Straight up: I'm changing careers and don't have three years in HVAC. What I do have is seven years running my own field service business with 5.0-star reviews, years working on construction and industrial job sites, and a clean driving record. I'm working on my EPA 608. If service needs more time in the trade, I'd take an install or apprentice spot to learn it right.

## Notes

- **Never hide the experience gap.** In the trades it comes out in the first technical question, and getting caught costs you the shop's respect permanently. Leading with it costs you nothing and buys credibility.
- **Always ask about install or apprentice work.** That is the realistic opening, and asking shows you understand the trade's structure.
- Mention the EPA 608 only once you have actually started studying for it.
- Call in the morning. Service managers are buried by afternoon.
