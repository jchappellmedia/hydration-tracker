Company research

Valleywise Health (formerly Maricopa Integrated Health System) is Maricopa County's public health system and Arizona's only public teaching hospital. It opened a new $935 million medical center on East Roosevelt Street in Phoenix in 2024. It also runs behavioral health centers in Mesa, Phoenix and Maryvale, a large health center in Peoria, and community health centers across the Valley. Headcount estimates from outside sources disagree a lot (Revelio about 1,800, PitchBook about 1,600, Glassdoor 1,001-5,000), so treat them as rough. All the reviews found cover the whole company and come mostly from clinical staff such as nurses, behavioral health techs and ED staff. No reviews from the Marketing & Communications team, which this job sits in, turned up. Many Glassdoor reviews are old (some date from 2014 to 2021), so it is safer to rely on the recurring themes than on any single review.

Glassdoor

3.5 / 5 (About 320-340 company-wide reviews (snapshots show 322, 331, 333 and 339). Recommend-to-a-friend is 52%-63% depending on the snapshot. One snapshot shows 73% CEO approval.) Mixed reviews. What employees say: pros and cons

Pros

  • Strong retirement and benefits. The employer matches 100% of the Arizona State Retirement System (ASRS) pension contribution. Medical plans are said to cover more than 75% of costs. There are also 457(b) Roth and 401(a) plans, dental and vision, life insurance and an employee assistance program. Glassdoor reviewers often name benefits and retirement as a top pro. [1] [2] [3]
  • Meaningful public-health mission. It is Arizona's only public teaching hospital and serves many underserved patients. 'Some really great people and a worthwhile mission' is a recurring Glassdoor theme. [1] [2]
  • Good coworkers and supportive managers on some teams. Reviewers say managers have their backs, and an April 2026 Indeed review describes a supportive culture and an amazing team. This depends on the department. [1] [2]
  • New, modern main campus. The $935 million Valleywise Health Medical Center in Phoenix opened in 2024, along with a new 126,000 sq ft health center in Peoria. That means new facilities and a lot of new services to show in media. [1] [2]
  • The hiring process is reported as fairly quick and simple: about two weeks on average, rated easy, with a phone screen, a manager interview and a background check. [1]
  • Some reviewers mention flexible schedules or hours. Only a few reviews say this, so it is a small sample. [1] [2]

Cons

  • Pay is the most common complaint. Reviewers say salaries are lower than at other Phoenix healthcare systems and call the public-sector pay formula 'tricky'. [1] [2]
  • Senior leadership gets poor marks. It is the lowest-rated Glassdoor category (about 2.6-2.8 / 5), with recurring complaints about 'lack of accountability from senior leadership' and a 'top heavy' organization. [1] [2] [3]
  • A mandatory ASRS pension contribution of about 12% comes out of every paycheck, which lowers take-home pay. Several reviewers say take-home pay was less than they expected. [1] [2]
  • Limited career growth and upskilling are a repeated con in recent reviews. [1] [2]
  • Staffing shortages and heavy workloads come up in reviews, including a January 2026 Glassdoor review. Local news has also covered behavioral-health nurse shortages. Most of this concerns clinical units, not marketing. [1] [2]
  • Management quality varies by department. For example, an Indeed ED review describes favoritism, schedules changed without notice and required meetings on days off. Another reviewer raised concerns about building upkeep and staff safety. [1]

Watch for

  • No layoffs, lawsuits or 2026 scandals involving Valleywise Health turned up. The layoff and lawsuit results were about Valley Medical Center in Renton, WA, a different organization.
  • Senior leadership is consistently the lowest-rated area on Glassdoor. It is worth asking how the Sr VP of Marketing & Communications runs the team and sets priorities.
  • No reviews from the Marketing & Communications team were found. Every review is company-wide and mostly from clinical staff.
  • Minor: a May 2026 procurement document says an HR/payroll conversion to Workday was set to start in July 2026, so a new hire may go through some system changes.

Bottom line

Valleywise is a middle-of-the-road employer: 3.5/5 on both Glassdoor and Indeed, with most reviews from clinical staff. The strongest reasons to work there are the public-health mission, a new Phoenix campus and a good benefits package with a fully matched ASRS pension. The common complaints are below-market pay, a mandatory 12% pension deduction that lowers take-home pay, and weak senior leadership. Nothing serious (layoffs, lawsuits) turned up. Because no marketing-team reviews exist, Joshua should ask in the interview about team size, workload, how video requests get prioritized, and what take-home pay looks like after the ASRS deduction.
