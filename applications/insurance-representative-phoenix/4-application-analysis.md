# Application Analysis & Feedback — Insurance Representative, Phoenix

## Job Match: Strong on profile, one hard requirement to clear

Like Edward Jones and WhyGen, this posting screens for drive, self-direction, and relationship sales — and your six years as a business owner is direct evidence. They prefer a business-related degree (you exceed it with a marketing Master's) and prefer but don't require insurance sales experience. Where this one differs:

### The license requirement is a hard gate
**"Life and health insurance license is required."** You don't have one. What that means in practice:
- Arizona requires a pre-licensing course (or self-study), then the state Life & Health exam, then fingerprinting. Most people finish in 2–4 weeks; total cost is roughly $200–$400.
- Many career insurance agencies interview and extend conditional offers to unlicensed candidates, then require you to obtain the license before your start date. Some sponsor or reimburse the cost — ask.
- If you're serious about this path (or Edward Jones), starting an AZ Life & Health pre-licensing course now strengthens both applications. Common providers: Kaplan, ExamFX, XCEL.

### What kind of job this is — read before committing
The posting is unusually honest: "The primary aspect of the position is to sell," service exists to create sales opportunities, and evenings and weekends are expected. Compensation isn't stated but this rep model is typically commission-heavy with small financing/allowances early. The metrics culture is intense: weekly application counts, persistency rates, lapse limits, supervised action-planning meetings. It's the same bet as WhyGen but in a heavily managed structure, versus Edward Jones which offers salaried ramp-up and a stronger long-term brand. If you're choosing among your three sales applications, Edward Jones remains the best risk-adjusted version of this career.
