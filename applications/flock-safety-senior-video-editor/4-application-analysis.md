## Fit: Strong. This is the best-matched posting in the tracker.

Every one of the five "What You'll Own" bullets describes something you have actually done, and the two modes the posting cares about are the exact two halves of your record:

- **Cinematic documentary — real people, emotional restraint.** The veterans film for the bath remodel company. The construction-into-cinema work. Cinematic corporate documentaries and interviews at Lightbox. This is not adjacent experience; it is the same work.
- **High-octane social-first.** 20x engagement on one initiative, 200% click-through lift on another, 100%+ paid and organic engagement. Very few editors can put numbers on the social half.

And the post-production stack they list — color grading, sound design, audio leveling, motion graphics integration, asset organization — you own all five, with measurable results behind two of them (Frame.io library at 50% faster retrieval, grading pipeline rebuilt to run 3x faster).

**One real hit on their bonus list:** they name Runway, and Runway is genuinely in your AI toolkit. Name it. Do not claim LucidLink, Twelve Labs, or Figma Weave — those are not yours.

## The single most important thing in this application

> *"A reel that shows emotional range, narrative intelligence, and technical finish — not simply footage assembled cleanly to music."*

That sentence is a warning, and it is aimed at exactly the kind of reel most editors send. **Check your demo reel before you submit.** If vimeo.com/1103241704 is a music-driven montage of best shots, it will read as the thing they just told you not to send — regardless of how good the shots are.

What to do instead: send the reel *plus* one or two complete narrative pieces, with a line of context on each.

- **The veterans piece** (the Frame.io presentation) — this is your strongest single asset for this job. Real people, real stakes, emotional restraint. It is the posting's first paragraph in film form.
- **Construction into cinema** (vimeo.com/1099054535) — technical finish and visual pacing on an unglamorous subject, which is close to what turning public-safety technology into story requires.

If they only watch one thing, you want it to be the veterans film, not a montage.

## The honest gaps

1. **They ask for 8+ years; you have 7+.** On paper you are a year short. It is a soft filter at most companies and your body of work is deep for the time, but do not round up to 8 — if a recruiter checks, an inflated number costs you more than the year ever would. If asked, "seven-plus years, and 200+ delivered videos in that time" is the right answer.
2. **LucidLink, Twelve Labs, Figma Weave.** Left off entirely. They are listed as a plus, not a requirement, and two of the three are tools you would learn in a week.
3. **No technology-product content.** Flock sells surveillance and investigative tools to police departments. You have not marketed a technical product, and the subject matter carries weight that a hiring manager may probe.

## Two things to think about, separate from qualifications

**This is a post-only role.** The posting says plainly: *"This isn't a field production or camera operator position."* Producers and crews shoot; you stay in the edit. You are a shooter as well as an editor, and this job asks you to set the camera down. For some people that is a relief and a chance to go deep on craft. For others it is losing half the job they love. Worth knowing which you are before the interview, because they will ask why you want a post-only seat.

**Location is the same open question as the other Flock role.** They prioritize Atlanta and Boston, and they state that remote postings say so — this one does not. The cover letter carries your standard "Willing to relocate" line. **If that is not true, say so and I will pull it from both Flock letters.**

## Compensation

$100,000–$120,000 plus stock options. Comfortably above your $75k floor, and realistic for the level — this is a senior individual-contributor editing role, not department leadership. With seven years, a Master's, and the measurable workflow results, the upper half of the band is a fair target. As always, the number is yours to give, not mine.

## Interview talking points

- **Lead with the veterans film.** Walk them through the choices: what you cut, what you held, where you let silence sit. That conversation demonstrates "narrative intelligence" better than any list of credits.
- **The two-modes answer.** Most editors are documentary people who tolerate social, or social people who cannot sit still long enough for documentary. You can show numbers on both. Say that directly.
- **First dedicated editor is a systems job too.** You have built the asset library, the storyboard process, and the grading pipeline from nothing before. That is what a first hire actually needs to do in month one, and it is your quiet advantage.
- **On the eight-year line**, be straightforward and move to the work.

## Bottom line

Apply, and prioritize this above the Flock Creative Director posting. That one was a reach on level. This one you would walk into and do well from week one.

Two conditions: confirm the relocation question, and **fix the reel problem before you hit submit.** The reel is the application here — the resume just gets it opened.
