# Application Question Answers — Flock Safety

Written to match Joshua's own voice: short declarative sentences, strong verbs
(crafted, delivered, built, slashed), confident without being wordy. Read it out
loud before pasting — if a line does not sound like you, change it.

## "Why do you want to work at Flock? What excites you most about the company and this role?"

### Primary answer (~195 words)

I want this job for the work. It owns the edit start to finish, and it asks for both things I do: documentary storytelling about real people, and fast social cuts that have to grab attention in the first second. Most editing jobs want one or the other. This one wants both, and I have delivered on both — a social initiative that earned more than 20x the engagement of our standard content, and documentaries clients still bring up to me.

What pulls me toward Flock is the subject. An investigation that closed. A family that got someone back. My best work has always come from real people with something on the line. I crafted a film for a remodeling company about how they care for veterans, and it landed because I let the veterans tell it instead of getting in the way.

I also caught the line in your posting about reels that are not just footage cut to music. That told me you care about craft as much as I do. Being your first dedicated editor means building the standard and the workflow from scratch, and I have built a post workflow from nothing before.

## Notes on answering this one

- **Do not say "I am passionate about public safety."** Everyone writes that. It reads as filler. The specific details — the veterans film, the 20x number, the line you caught in their posting — are what make it sound like a person wrote it.
- **Quoting their posting back works.** The "not footage assembled cleanly to music" line proves you read it closely and care about the same things. Most applicants skim.
- **Be ready for the follow-up.** Flock builds license plate readers and investigative tools for police departments. A recruiter may ask how you feel about that work. Have an honest answer in your own words — a rehearsed one will sound rehearsed. If you have reservations, that is worth knowing before you take a job telling this company's stories for a living.
