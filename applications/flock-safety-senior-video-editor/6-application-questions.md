# Application Question Answers — Flock Safety

## "Why do you want to work at Flock? What excites you most about the company and this role?"

### Primary answer (~200 words — use this unless the box is smaller)

I want the work itself first. This role owns the edit from rough cut to final delivery, in both of the modes I already work in: patient documentary storytelling about real people, and fast social-first cuts that have to earn attention in the first second. Most editing jobs ask for one or the other. This one asks for both, and I have results on both sides — a social initiative that earned more than 20x the engagement of our standard content, and documentary brand films that people still bring up to me.

What draws me to Flock specifically is the subject matter. An investigation that closed. A family that got someone back. My best work has always come from ordinary people with something real at stake. I cut a film for a remodeling company about how they care for veterans, and it worked because I stayed out of the way and let the people in it carry the story. That is the restraint your posting describes.

I also noticed the line about reels that are not just footage assembled to music. That told me the craft bar here is real. Being your first dedicated editor means setting that bar and building the systems behind it, and I have built a post workflow from nothing before.

## Notes on answering this one

- **Do not say "I am passionate about public safety."** Everyone writes that, and it reads as filler. The specific detail — the veterans film, the 20x number, the line you noticed in their posting — is what makes an answer sound like a person.
- **Quoting their own posting back works.** The "not footage assembled cleanly to music" line proves you read it closely and that you care about the same things. Most applicants skim.
- **Be ready for the follow-up.** Flock builds license plate readers and investigative tools for police departments. A recruiter may ask how you feel about that work. Have an honest answer ready in your own words — I am not going to write a conviction for you, and a rehearsed one will sound rehearsed. If you have reservations, that is worth knowing before you take a job telling this company's stories for a living.
- Keep your own voice. Read it out loud before pasting; if a sentence does not sound like you, change it.
