# Application Analysis — Ballot Chaser, Turning Point Action

## Fit: Partial — and there are four things to settle before you send anything

You clear most of the personal-quality bar easily. Self-starter, self-motivated, punctual, goal-oriented, comfortable working alone against a number: that is a description of running your own company for six years. Google Sheets, Facebook, Instagram, and X are all documented. Driver license, transportation, and willingness to pass a background check are all fine. Team management, listed as a "WOW" skill, you have — teams of 5 to 20 at Lightbox.

But four things are unresolved, and three of them are not about your qualifications.

### 1. The pay is not in the posting, and that is the biggest issue

Nothing in the text you sent names a wage, a salary, or a pay structure. It says goals are "attainable and incentivized," which usually signals hourly pay plus per-target bonuses.

Your floor is $75,000. Ballot chase field roles are, as a category, hourly and seasonal — the kind of work that pays a rate per hour rather than an annual salary. **I am not going to guess a number for you**, but you should assume this pays a fraction of the $85,000–$100,000 role you queued this morning until someone tells you otherwise in writing.

Ask before you invest any more time: what is the hourly rate, how many hours per week, and what do the incentives actually pay out?

### 2. It is almost certainly seasonal

Ballot chasing exists to move ballots before an election. The general election is November 3, 2026 — roughly eight weeks out. Programs like this staff up in the fall and wind down after the count.

Ask directly whether this runs past November, and what happens to the role afterward. If it is a two-month job, that is fine as long as you know it going in and it is not replacing a full-time search.

### 3. You may not be eligible, and neither of us can tell yet

The posting says: *"Must reside within three miles of the zip code on job posting."* The zip code was not in the text you pasted. Three miles is a tight radius — a few square miles.

Send me the original listing or the zip code and I can tell you in a second whether Mesa 85206 qualifies. Until then this is a hard gate we cannot clear.

### 4. "Unmatched passion for conservative politics" — that one is yours to answer

This is a stated minimum qualification, and it is a real one for an organization like this. They will ask about it in an interview, and a field manager will know within a few minutes whether the answer is genuine.

**I deliberately left that claim out of your cover letter.** I write from your actual record, and your convictions are not something I will put on paper for you. If it is true, say it in your own words and the letter is easy to strengthen — tell me and I will add it. If it is not, this is not a job to talk your way into, because the work is eight weeks of knocking on doors and asking strangers to act on that conviction.

## The one qualification gap

**No field staff or grassroots campaign experience.** That is listed first among the minimum qualifications. "Or relevant experience" gives you room, and your closest real claim is the Arizona State Legislature work — but be precise about it in an interview. You produced video in a legislative environment and coordinated with Communications teams across chambers. You were not organizing voters. Overstating that is the fastest way to lose credibility with someone who has actually run a field program.

What genuinely transfers, and what I would lead with:

- Six years of self-generated leads and door-level selling of your own services
- 90+ students a year mentored, plus a community event you organized from scratch
- Weekly goal tracking in spreadsheets, which is literally the daily reporting this job describes
- 98% on-time delivery — the punctuality and responsiveness they ask for, with a number attached

## One professional consideration, separate from politics

Field work for a named political organization is public in a way your media work is not. Your name, in a partisan role, becomes searchable and attached to that organization. For some future employers that is neutral or a plus; for others in corporate media and marketing it is a data point. That is not an argument against it — plenty of people do political work and move on fine — but it is worth deciding on purpose rather than discovering later.

## Interview talking points

- **Lead with the spreadsheet discipline.** "I set revenue goals and tracked them weekly for six years" is exactly the behavior a Ballot Chase Manager needs and cannot teach.
- **The door-to-door parallel is real.** You sold your own services to homeowners and realtors. That is the same muscle as a doorstep conversation with a stranger.
- **Community organizing, small scale.** The gallery night you built at American Leadership Academy is a legitimate community-organizing story: you got families in a room around something they cared about.
- **Be straight about the field gap.** "I have not run a field program. I have run my own business against weekly targets for six years, and I learn fast." That answer holds up. A padded one will not.

## Bottom line

The personal qualities line up well and you would likely do the job competently. But right now this is an application with no known pay, an unknown end date, an eligibility gate we cannot check, and a conviction requirement only you can speak to.

**Get the zip code and the pay first.** If the zip code rules you out, nothing else matters. If it does not, and the pay and duration work for what you want right now, the materials are built and ready to send.
