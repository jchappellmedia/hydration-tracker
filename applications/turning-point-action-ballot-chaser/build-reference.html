Turning Point Action is a 501(c)(4) organization founded in 2019 by Charlie Kirk. The organization’s mission is to embolden the conservative base through grassroots activism and provide voters with the necessary resources to elect true conservative leaders. Turning Point Action has emerged as the country’s leading grassroots organization, giving individuals the opportunity and platform to run for office at the local level, providing voters with critical information surrounding their district’s candidates and elections, allowing them unparalleled access to some of the most impactful figures in the movement. Turning Point Action is on a mission to save America, one precinct at a time.

To learn more about the organization, founder Charlie Kirk, and its vision for a freer America, visit tpaction.com.

Job Description: Turning Point Action is seeking a highly skilled, natural born leader and motivated individual to join TPAction’s innovative and large Chase The Vote initiative. Day-to-day responsibilities include daily communication with the ballot chase manager, building inter-community relationships, and encouraging others to send in their ballots. Responsibilities also include building a community network system where chasers identify and monitor low propensity voting habits in precincts across a designated territory. The Ballot Chaser has attainable and incentivized goals and works in tandem with the Ballot Chase Manager in order to make sure that goals are reached, community relationships are fostered and data is tracked daily.

Company research

Turning Point Action (TPA) is a 501(c)(4) political advocacy group founded in 2019 by Charlie Kirk as the sister organization and political arm of Turning Point USA (TPUSA). Its headquarters is in Phoenix. It runs the "Chase the Vote" ballot-chase program, and in 2024 the Trump campaign handed it parts of its Arizona field work. Revelio Labs, a third-party estimate, puts TPA at about 105 employees in March 2026 and counts 353 job postings in 2026, up 173.5% from 2025. No employee review profile for TPA turned up on Glassdoor, Indeed, Comparably, CareerBliss, or Kununu. Most "Turning Point" review pages belong to unrelated employers, such as a UK health charity and an Indian retailer. The closest data comes from TPUSA, the sister 501(c)(3), and every review figure below is labeled as TPUSA. Those reviews cover the whole company (117 on Glassdoor, 37 on Indeed). None of them is specific to the media team or to ballot chasing. About the two roles: Ballot Chase Representative postings are tied to individual ZIP codes, and they include Mesa ones such as "Mesa High" and "Enid Park (85206)". The work is contacting low-turnout voters in an assigned precinct and reporting daily to a ballot chase manager. The postings require a driver's license, reliable transportation, and a background check, and some require living within 3 miles of the posted ZIP code. The Media Administrator role (Phoenix, full-time) appears only as a Talent.com aggregator snippet, with no duties, pay, or posting date shown.

What employees say: pros and cons

Pros

  • Reviewers describe it as a good first job in conservative politics, especially for recent graduates (TPUSA reviews). [1] [2]
  • Some 5-star reviews praise the HQ culture, work-life balance, flexibility, and the mission (TPUSA reviews). [1] [2]
  • TPA is growing fast, which means openings and room to move up. One estimate counts 353 postings in 2026, up 173.5% from 2025, with about 105 staff (TPA, third-party estimate). [1]
  • The headquarters is in Phoenix, a short commute from Mesa, and ballot chase postings exist for Mesa ZIP codes such as Mesa High and Enid Park (85206) (TPA). [1] [2] [3]
  • The work is high-profile, which can help a resume in political media and campaign work. TPA ran a large part of the 2024 Trump ground game in Arizona and has been credited with 2026 primary results in Indiana (TPA). [1] [2] [3]
  • Indeed's work wellbeing score is 71 from 39 responses, a middling result rather than a warning sign (TPUSA). [1]

Cons

  • Management is the most common complaint. Glassdoor's summary counts 'poor management' in 17 reviews and 'disorganized leadership / shifting priorities' in 7, and some reviewers describe unprofessional or passive-aggressive managers (TPUSA). [1] [2]
  • Long hours and after-hours availability. Work-life balance averages 3.3/5, 'no work/life balance' appears in several reviews, and current staff mention early mornings, late nights, and a lot of travel (TPUSA). [1] [2]
  • Pay is low for the workload. Compensation is rated 3.2/5, and reviewers say the workload is heavy relative to pay (TPUSA). [1] [2]
  • Turnover is high. One former administrator called it 'insane', and another said hiring and departures happened at the same pace (TPUSA). [1] [2]
  • Turmoil since Charlie Kirk's killing in September 2025. Former staff allege loyalty checks followed by firings, retaliation against a PR manager who questioned leadership, and 20-hour workdays. These claims are disputed and not independently verified (TPUSA). [1] [2] [3]
  • Older allegations of a 'frat-boy' party culture, misconduct at events, and cover-ups. Sourcing for these varies (TPUSA). [1] [2]
  • Ballot chase jobs follow the election calendar and are tied to one ZIP code. They need your own car, a license, and a background check, and some require living within 3 miles of the territory. The postings seen do not say how long the job lasts (TPA). [1] [2]

Watch for

  • No employee reviews exist for Turning Point Action itself. Every rating and quote here is from Turning Point USA, a separate legal entity, and covers the whole company rather than the media or field teams.
  • Former staff say people were questioned about loyalty and then fired after September 2025, and a PR manager says she was dismissed for asking questions. These claims are disputed and not verified, but they are recent and touch the media/PR side.
  • A Glassdoor reviewer claimed management pushes staff to post positive reviews. This is not verified, but if true the 3.6 rating would be inflated.
  • TPUSA's Glassdoor page still lists Charlie Kirk as CEO, so its CEO-approval number (74%) does not reflect current leadership.
  • The Media Administrator listing was seen only as a Talent.com snippet with no date, duties, or pay. Confirm on tpaction.com/careers that it is still open.
  • Many Glassdoor and Indeed 'Turning Point' pages, rated about 2.7 to 3.4, belong to unrelated employers such as a UK health charity. Do not mix them with TPA or TPUSA.

Bottom line

There is no direct review data for Turning Point Action, so everything here comes from its sister group, TPUSA. TPUSA's ratings are middling: 3.6/5 on Glassdoor from 117 reviews and 3.1/5 on Indeed from 37. Reviews are mixed. Positives are the mission, the culture for young staff, and value as a first job in politics. Negatives are management, long hours, pay, and turnover. On top of that, there are unverified reports of loyalty-based firings since late 2025. TPA is hiring heavily for 2026, and both roles are in Mesa or Phoenix, so the jobs look real and close to home. For Ballot Chaser, ask how long the job lasts, what the weekly hours are, and whether mileage is reimbursed. For Media Administrator, ask who the role reports to, how big the media team is, what after-hours or weekend coverage is expected, and how much staff turnover the team has had since 2025.
