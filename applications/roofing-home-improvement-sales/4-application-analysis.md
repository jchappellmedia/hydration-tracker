# Application Analysis & Feedback — Roofing & Home Improvement Sales

## Job Match: Strong for what they screen for — but read the flags first

On their stated criteria (coachable, hungry, reliable transportation, no resume required), you clear the bar easily, and you bring two things most applicants won't have:
1. **You already know their industry.** You've filmed and marketed for construction companies, home developers, and a bath remodel company. You can talk roofing and remodels with a homeowner without faking it.
2. **You've lived on commission.** Six years of business ownership is six years of eat-what-you-kill. The summary and cover letter say this in their own language.

The Spartan race made the resume this time — this ad literally sells heat tolerance and grit, and that line is proof.

## Honest flags — bigger here than any job so far

1. **The company won't say its name.** That is unusual and it prevents the most basic diligence: checking their ROC license, BBB record, and Google reviews. Before any commitment, get the legal company name and look up their Arizona Registrar of Contractors license (roc.az.gov) and reviews. A legitimate roofer has both.
2. **The income claims are recruiting copy.** "Reps average $200K+," "$150K is our low end," "$5K/week in 90 days" — no company publishes verified numbers like this. In 100% commission door sales, the top decile can earn large amounts; the median often earns far less, and turnover is extreme. The ad even admits it: "we'll both know within the first couple days."
3. **100% commission almost certainly means 1099 contractor.** No base, no benefits, self-employment taxes, and likely chargebacks if a customer cancels. Ask directly: W-2 or 1099, what's the commission structure, when do you get paid, and what happens on cancellations.
4. **Storm-chasing dynamics.** "Knocking the neighborhood around our active job site" is standard and fine; but roofing door sales in general has a segment with high-pressure tactics. Judge the company by how their reps talk to homeowners on your shadow day.

## The smart play
Their one-day shadow offer is genuinely the right move — for you, not just them. It costs a day and answers everything: how reps actually pitch, how many doors per appointment, how many appointments per sale, and what the middle-of-the-pack rep (not the star) really earns. Take the shadow day before giving any commitment. Ask a rep who's been there 6+ months — not 98 days — what their last three months actually paid.

## Comparison across your four sales-track options
- **Edward Jones:** salaried ramp, licensing paid, durable career. Best long-term.
- **Valleywise:** salaried W-2 with benefits in your actual profession. Best stability.
- **WhyGen Solar / this roofing role:** same bet as each other — commission-only door sales. Roofing has one edge over solar for you: you already know the trades world. But both are the highest-risk options on your board.

## Bottom line
Applying costs nothing and the shadow day is worth doing for the education alone. Just don't quit anything, sign anything, or turn down other offers until you've seen the middle-rep reality and verified the company's name, license, and reviews.
