# Application Analysis — Creative Director, Flock Safety

## Fit: Weak to Partial. This is a reach, and you should know that going in.

I built the materials because you sent it, and they are good materials. But I am not going to tell you this is a strong application, because it is not. Here is the honest version.

**What this role actually is.** Creative Director owning brand, film, and motion at a 1,700-person company with over $1B raised, paying $200,000–$250,000 plus equity. Hires at that level usually come with twelve to fifteen years of experience, several of them running a creative department or an agency team, with discipline leads reporting in. You have seven-plus years, most of it as an owner-operator and an individual contributor with excellent craft.

That is not a knock on your work. It is a level difference.

## Where you genuinely match

Three of their five "What You Bring" bullets, you can defend:

- **Full production lifecycle fluency.** Editing pace, color grading, finishing — real and deep. Your DaVinci and log-grading work is legitimately strong, and the 3x faster grading pipeline at the Legislature is exactly the kind of thing they are asking for.
- **Operational systems that improve delivery speed.** This is your quietest strength and their fourth ownership bullet. Frame.io library cutting retrieval 50%, storyboard process cutting revisions 50%, AI workflows halving production time. Most creative directors cannot show numbers like that.
- **Narrative documentary and commercial storytelling.** You have it, with range — construction, a veterans story, higher ed, government.

## Where you do not match, and they will notice

1. **"Leading multidisciplinary creative teams across art direction, design, motion, and cinematic film."** You have directed *production crews* of 5 to 20, and you led *within* a 15-person media team at Grand Canyon Education — you did not lead it. You have never managed discipline leads in art direction and design. This is their first bullet, and it is the biggest gap.
2. **"Deep portfolio demonstrating high-craft visual design."** You are a filmmaker, not a designer. Your motion graphics work in After Effects is real, but a design portfolio — layout, identity, systems — is not something your record supports. They will ask for it.
3. **"Typography."** Named explicitly. Not in your record at all.
4. **"Setting practical brand guidelines, design systems, and creative review frameworks."** No documented experience owning a brand system or a design system. You have built *production* systems, which is adjacent but not the same thing, and a hiring manager will know the difference.
5. **"Clear technical product messaging."** You have never marketed a technology product. Flock sells surveillance and investigative tools to police departments — a subject that requires real care and specific experience.

I left every one of these off the resume. Nothing on that page is padded.

## The location problem, which may end this before anything else

The posting says Flock prioritizes Atlanta and Boston, that hub-based roles mean real in-person time, and — this is the key sentence — **"when a posting is open to remote work, it says so."** This posting does not say so.

So this is almost certainly a relocation to Atlanta or Boston. The cover letter says "Willing to relocate," per your standard template. **If that is not actually true, tell me and I will pull that line immediately** — it is the kind of claim that wastes everyone's time if you do not mean it.

## Why it might still be worth sending

- **The pay is roughly three times your floor.** $200–250k base plus equity at a company with $1B raised. Even a low-probability shot at that is worth an hour.
- **Applications are cheap and the downside is zero.** Nobody blacklists you for aiming high.
- **The systems angle is a genuinely differentiated pitch.** Plenty of creative directors have taste. Far fewer can say they cut retrieval time 50% and production time in half with numbers attached. If anything gets you a first conversation, it is that.
- **The recruiter screen is where you learn things.** Even a "no" conversation tells you what this tier of role wants, which sharpens the next one.

## If you get a conversation, here is how to handle the gap

Do not pretend to be a creative director who has run a department. Say this instead:

> "I have owned concept through delivery on brand films and campaigns for seven years, directed crews of up to twenty, and built the production systems that make a team faster. I have not managed discipline leads in design and art direction — that would be the step up for me, and I would want to be straight with you about it."

That answer loses you the roles you were never getting and occasionally wins you one, because it reads as someone with judgment. Overclaiming loses you the room the moment they ask a follow-up about typography or design systems.

## Realistic alternative

If you want the Flock-tier pay in a role you would actually clear, the titles to search are **Senior Video Producer, Brand Film Lead, Content Production Lead, or Video Team Lead** at companies this size. Those often pay $120k–$180k, sit squarely inside your record, and are a legitimate stepping stone to the job in this posting. Say the word and I will run that search.

## Bottom line

Send it if the relocation is genuinely on the table for you — the materials are ready and the upside is large. Do not count on it, do not let it slow down the roles where you are the obvious hire, and do not claim the design and team-leadership experience in an interview. The honest version of your story is strong enough to be interesting at this level even when it is not enough to win.
