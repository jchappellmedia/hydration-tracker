  • He builds systems that speed up delivery, with numbers: a Frame.io library that cut retrieval time 50%, a storyboard process that cut revision rounds 50%, and AI workflows that cut production time in half.

Gaps to be ready for

Suggested answers carried over from the original analysis. Some assume a course or certification is under way; say those only if they are true when you interview.

  • He has never led teams that include art direction and design leads; he directed production crews and worked within a 15-person team. Answer: 'I've owned concept through delivery for seven years and directed crews of up to twenty. I haven't managed design and art-direction leads. That would be the step up for me.'
  • No design portfolio (layout, identity, design systems); he is a filmmaker. Answer: 'My portfolio is film and motion. My design work is motion graphics in After Effects, not identity or layout systems.'
  • Typography is not in his record at all. Answer: 'I haven't owned typography standards. That's where I'd lean on a strong design lead.'
  • He has never owned brand guidelines or a design system. Answer: 'I've built production systems: asset libraries, a storyboard process, a grading pipeline. That's adjacent to brand systems, not the same thing, and it's where I'd add the most.'
  • No experience marketing a tech product, and Flock's subject matter is sensitive. Answer: 'I haven't marketed a tech product. My strength is finding the human story, and I'd partner with product marketing on the technical side.'

The Problem Flock's creative group is expanding as the company raises its standard of visual storytelling and campaign execution across brand, film, and digital experiences. This momentum creates a need for unified creative leadership to turn complex technology and public safety outcomes into compelling, human-centered narratives. You will own that direction, driving the creative concept, visual identity, and craft bar across design, film, and motion.

What You'll Own

  • Direct integrated campaigns, product launch narratives, and flagship brand films from initial concept through final delivery.
  • Define and maintain the visual, cinematic, and editorial standards across design, motion, and video production to build a recognizable creative signature.
  • Lead and mentor multidisciplinary craft teams, guiding discipline leads in art direction, storytelling, and creative critique.
  • Establish operational systems, including reference libraries, templates, and production guidelines, that elevate quality while improving delivery speed.
  • Partner with brand strategy and production leadership to translate executive goals into clear creative briefs and execution plans.

What This Role is Not

  • This is not a hands-off review role, you will actively shape concepts, guide edits, direct art execution, and refine details across all mediums.
  • This isn't a high-volume template production management job, your main focus is establishing concept quality and narrative depth.
  • This is not an isolated brand strategy position, you will work directly alongside production and cross-functional teams to ship live campaigns.

What You Bring

  • Proven track record leading multidisciplinary creative teams across art direction, design, motion, and cinematic film production.
  • Deep portfolio demonstrating high-craft visual design alongside narrative documentary or high-paced commercial storytelling.
  • Advanced fluency in the complete production lifecycle, including typography, editing pace, color grading, sound design, and finishing standards.
  • Experience setting practical brand guidelines, design systems, and creative review frameworks for internal teams and agency partners.
  • Ability to direct campaigns that balance emotional human narrative with clear technical product messaging.

Company research

Flock Safety is an Atlanta-based company that makes license plate reader cameras and related public safety software sold to police departments, cities, and businesses. Recent press puts it at about 1,500 employees and an $8B valuation, and it is described as remote-first. Both roles Joshua applied for sit in Marketing. The Creative Director role (Remote - USA / Los Angeles, listed at $200K-$250K) owns the visual identity and leads brand films, campaigns, and launch stories. The posting says it is "not a hands-off review role." The Senior Video Editor role (Film & Editorial, Remote - USA, $100K-$120K plus stock options) would be Flock's first full-time dedicated editor. It covers documentary-style, brand, and product video. The company is going through a hard period. In October 2026, Reuters reported plans to cut about 270 jobs (about 18% of staff) after a voluntary buyout program. This comes amid public backlash over surveillance and a wave of city contract cancellations. I found no reviews specific to the creative or film team. All ratings below are company-wide, and many of the negative reviews come from sales roles.

What employees say: pros and cons

Pros

  • Reviewers across sites name the mission and the people as the main reasons they like the job. They describe coworkers as talented and the culture as supportive. This feedback is company-wide, not specific to the creative team. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Both posted salaries are clear and competitive for remote roles. Creative Director lists $200K-$250K. Senior Video Editor lists $100K-$120K plus stock options. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • The creative group is being built, which gives a new hire room to shape it. Flock has posted a Creative Director, its first full-time dedicated video editor, a Creative Manager (player-coach), and a Senior Creative Operations Producer for brand and video projects. The Creative Director posting calls for hands-on work on concepts and edits, not only review. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • The company is described as remote-first, and both roles are listed as Remote - USA. That works for someone based in Mesa, Arizona. [1] [2] [3]
  • Pay and benefits score highest of Indeed's categories (3.7). Reviewers mention flexible or unlimited PTO, fertility benefits, and an equipment and home-office start-up bonus. Blind's overall rating is 4.4, but it rests on only 18-19 reviews. [1] [2] [3]
  • Some reviewers value the autonomy and the chance to take on new projects. One Glassdoor review is titled 'Fast-paced company full of opportunity.' Flock was also on Built In's 2024 Best Places to Work list, though that list is older and predates the 2026 turmoil. [1] [2]

Cons

  • Flock is reducing staff right now. Reuters reported (Oct 8, 2026) plans to cut about 270 jobs, about 18% of roughly 1,500 staff, after a voluntary separation program that closed Oct 2. Departures are expected at the end of October, and WIRED's sources said layoffs would 'almost certainly' follow if too few people took the buyout. Nothing found says whether marketing or creative roles are affected. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • The company faces strong public backlash over surveillance and privacy. Concerns include police sharing Flock data with ICE. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 47% of Americans oppose Flock cameras in their communities. The CEO has said internal morale has taken the most damage. A brand film and editorial lead would be telling this company's story while it is under heavy public scrutiny, and the work would carry Flock's name in a portfolio. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Customers are leaving. Secure Justice counts at least 214 cities and counties that have dropped Flock since 2021, 90 of them in 2026. IBTimes reports 93 ended contracts in August alone. WIRED reports this could leave Flock short of its revenue goals. Flock says renewals outpace cancellations. [1] [2]
  • Reviewers describe a fast pace with constant change, heavy workloads, and too many meetings. One Indeed reviewer reported 75-hour weeks. A Glassdoor review is titled 'Terrible place to work - Overworked.' Indeed rates work-life balance 3.0, and Glassdoor rates it about 3.5-3.8. [1] [2] [3]
  • Management is the weakest-rated area. It scores 2.8 on Indeed. Glassdoor reviewers say people managers lack training, promotions are rare, turnover is high, and many senior leaders have never worked with the product or its end users. [1] [2] [3]
  • Some reviewers say pay lags the market. A RepVue review cites no 401K match and expensive healthcare, and compensation/benefits is Blind's lowest category (3.8). The PIP and quota complaints come mostly from sales staff, so they may not apply to marketing. [1] [2] [3]
  • The Senior Video Editor posting says Flock prioritizes candidates near Atlanta and Boston for in-person reasons, and the Creative Director listing names Los Angeles. A Mesa, Arizona applicant may rank behind local candidates even though both roles are listed as remote. [1] [2]

Watch for

  • Active staff reduction of about 270 roles (about 18%) as of October 2026, with involuntary layoffs reported as likely if the buyouts fall short. Confirm the creative roles are funded and were not affected before accepting an offer.
  • City and county contract cancellations are rising (90 in 2026 per Secure Justice, via Futurism), and WIRED reports this puts revenue goals at risk.
  • A privacy lawsuit in Virginia alleges the cameras amount to warrantless surveillance. One outlet reports a January 2026 federal ruling for Flock that is now on appeal, but I could not confirm this with a second source.
  • Wide public opposition to the product (47% opposed in a Reuters/Ipsos poll), plus reports of police sharing Flock data with ICE. The CEO says internal morale has suffered most. The brand and film roles would sit at the center of this.
  • Glassdoor has several pages named 'Flock'. Only E2121535 is Flock Safety, so the 4.7 and 3.8 ratings on other 'Flock' pages are not about this employer.
  • Ratings are inconsistent and the samples are small: Glassdoor about 3.9 (about 214 reviews), Indeed 3.1 (29 reviews), Blind 4.4 (18-19 reviews). None of the reviews found are from the creative or film team.

Bottom line

Both jobs fit Joshua on paper. They are remote, the pay is stated ($200K-$250K for Creative Director, $100K-$120K plus options for Senior Video Editor), and the creative group is new, so the first editor or creative lead would shape how it works. Company-wide reviews are mixed to good: people like the mission and their coworkers, and complain about pace, workload, and management. No reviews were found from the creative or film team. The larger risk is timing. Flock is cutting about 18% of staff in October 2026 while cities cancel contracts and public opposition grows. In interviews, ask: (1) Were these roles affected by the buyout or reduction, and are they budgeted for 2027? (2) Who leads marketing, and who would this role report to? (3) What is the film and production budget? (4) How does the brand team handle public criticism of the product? (5) Is an Arizona-based hire fine, given that the editor posting prioritizes Atlanta and Boston? Also decide whether you are comfortable having brand work for a surveillance company in your portfolio.
