# Application Analysis & Feedback — Financial Advisor, Edward Jones

## Job Match: Strong for their hiring profile

Edward Jones does not require finance experience for this role — they require proof you can build a business on relationships and discipline. You have exactly the profile they recruit:

- **Business owner, six years.** This is their favorite candidate type. You generated your own leads, closed your own deals, and lived on your own results — which is what the first five years of an advisor practice demands.
- **Trust signals.** A year-plus of 5.0-star reviews is a trust story, and trust is the product a financial advisor sells.
- **Relationship range.** Realtors, home developers, CEOs, executives, school families — you already network across the exact demographics that hold investable assets.
- **Financial discipline.** You set revenue goals and tracked expenses in Excel and Sheets. Small, but it answers "is this person comfortable with numbers."
- **Analytics degree.** Master's with a Marketing Analytics specialization signals you can pass the licensing exams — a real screen for them, since Series 7 failure is a common washout.
- **Community + teaching.** Two years teaching and a community gallery night support the community-presence story.

## Honest considerations before you commit
1. **This is a prospecting job, not an analysis job.** Year one is heavy outreach — networking, community events, and lots of no's. Your WhyGen-style hustle questions apply here too, except Edward Jones pays a salary during ramp-up, which makes it a much safer version of the same bet.
2. **The washout is real.** Industry-wide, a large share of new advisors don't reach year four. The ones who make it usually have deep local networks — you have that in the East Valley.
3. **Licensing gauntlet.** SIE + Series 7 + Series 66 in your first months, paid study time. Treat it like a job.
4. **Background/credit check.** FINRA registration includes fingerprints, background, and credit review — standard, just be ready.
5. **Career direction.** Unlike solar, this one builds a durable, compounding career with real long-term equity in your book of clients. Of your recent non-media applications, this is the stronger long-term play.

## Interview Prep
1. **"Why financial advising?"** Have a real answer. Best honest angle from your background: you've spent six years helping families make big financial decisions (homes, businesses) from the media side, and you want the client relationship to be the career, not the camera.
2. **"Walk me through how you'd build your book."** Answer with what you did at Lightbox: referrals from realtors and developers, community presence, Google Business, consistent follow-up.
3. **Day-in-the-life question is a filter.** They want to hear you're comfortable with rejection-heavy outreach.
4. Mention the Spartan race if grit comes up — it lands well with this audience too.
