Company research

Marcus & Millichap (NYSE: MMI) is a commercial real estate investment brokerage and financing firm. Its headquarters are in Calabasas, CA. It has 80+ offices and about 1,700 sales and financing professionals, who are mostly 100% commission agents and independent contractors. A smaller salaried corporate staff supports them. Andrew Strockis has been SVP / Chief Marketing Officer since November 2021. He came from Charles Schwab and oversees all corporate marketing and branding staff, and the company leadership page still lists him. Corporate marketing has posted fully remote roles recently: a Marketing Manager in Corporate Marketing (posted around December 2025, 5-10 years of experience, no pay listed) and a Senior Manager, Marketing Platforms & Production Solutions (around June 2026, $100k-$110k base). Finances: net loss of $12.4M in 2024, $1.9M in 2025 and $3.1M in Q1 2026. Q1 2026 revenue was $171.5M, and adjusted EBITDA turned positive. Most public employee reviews come from commission agents and brokers, not salaried corporate marketing staff. Marketing-specific evidence is limited to a Glassdoor Marketing Coordinator page (52 reviews, many likely from coordinators on broker teams), one 2016 Glassdoor Marketing Manager review, and one Indeed Marketing Manager review. The Indeed MCP tool failed to connect, so Indeed data comes from search results only.

What employees say: pros and cons

Pros

  • Corporate marketing hires remote roles. A Marketing Manager role in Corporate Marketing (around December 2025) was fully remote. A Senior Manager, Marketing Platforms & Production Solutions role (around June 2026) was also remote, with a posted base of $100k-$110k. That range is a useful comparison for the $85k-$100k band. [1] [2] [3]
  • Marketing leadership has been stable. CMO Andrew Strockis joined in November 2021 from Charles Schwab, oversees all corporate marketing and branding, and is still listed on the leadership page. Searches found no reports of a CMO departure. [1] [2]
  • Training and coworkers are the most-praised items. 'Training was great' appears in 121 Glassdoor reviews, and Indeed reviewers mention frequent training. These are mostly agent reviews. [1] [2]
  • Some marketing staff describe a good culture. An Indeed Marketing Manager in LA called the culture good. Glassdoor Marketing Coordinator reviewers praised culture and the team's use of Asana. A small CareerBliss sample from the Calabasas HQ (2 reviews) described a relaxed setting with a casual dress code. [1] [2] [3]
  • Salaried staff get standard benefits. Built In lists performance bonuses, health and dental insurance, and a 401(k). The 'no benefits' complaints on Glassdoor come mostly from commission agents. [1] [2]
  • Results are improving. The net loss fell from $12.4M (2024) to $1.9M (2025). In Q1 2026 revenue rose to $171.5M and adjusted EBITDA turned positive. The firm also won a GlobeSt Best Place to Work award two years in a row (per a 2024 release). [1] [2] [3]

Cons

  • Pay gets the lowest scores. Glassdoor compensation and benefits is 2.9 company-wide and 2.1 on the Marketing Coordinator page. RepVue rates base pay 1.8 out of 47 ratings, mostly from sales roles. Payscale estimates for a Marketing Manager in New York run $39k-$69k. These are estimates, not verified pay. [1] [2] [3]
  • Marketing staff report limited growth. The Glassdoor Marketing Coordinator page scores 3.3 from 52 reviews, with 23% recommending and complaints of no advancement. An Indeed Marketing Manager said the work became repetitive after a year. CareerBliss rates growth opportunities 3.2. [1] [2] [3]
  • Corporate staff, not brokers, have taken past cuts. In 2020 the firm cut about 20% of its salaried workforce. In December 2023 it reduced headcount again, and a spokesperson said the cuts were limited to corporate staff while the firm kept hiring agents. [1] [2]
  • Work-life balance and stress scores are below average. Glassdoor work-life balance is 3.2. Indeed rates stress-free below average. 'The management is terrible' appears in 32 Glassdoor reviews. The one Marketing Manager review (2016) cited a lack of organization at the management level. [1] [2] [3]
  • The company is still losing money in a slow deal market. It posted a net loss in 2024, 2025 and Q1 2026. In the Q2 2026 call, the CEO said deal timelines remain long and that headcount growth will come more slowly, through internships and fellowships. [1] [2]
  • The culture centers on brokers and varies by office. Indeed reviewers say each office is run by its own regional manager. Job postings describe a commission-driven, 'work hard, play hard' setting. Corporate marketing serves about 1,700 agents, which can mean many internal stakeholders with sales-first priorities. [1] [2]

Watch for

  • Past layoffs fell on salaried corporate staff: about 20% of salaried staff in 2020, and corporate-only cuts in December 2023. A corporate marketing role sits in that group.
  • Net losses continued through Q1 2026, although they are narrowing.
  • The Glassdoor Marketing Coordinator page shows only 23% would recommend (52 reviews, mixed and partly old, and many likely from coordinators on broker teams).
  • No company-wide remote or return-to-office policy was found, and some corporate roles are posted as in-office. Get the remote arrangement for this role in writing.
  • SEC filings flag a pending case (TwinRock Holdings v. Southside Ventures) with possible effects on the brokerage business. Searches did not confirm details or status.
  • Marketing-specific evidence is thin: one 2016 Marketing Manager review, one Indeed Marketing Manager review, and no Reddit discussion.

Bottom line

Company-wide ratings are average: Glassdoor 3.7 (about 1,100 reviews) and Indeed 3.4 (about 267 reviews). Both are driven mostly by commission agents, so they say little about salaried corporate marketing. The marketing-specific signals are weaker. They point to low pay scores, limited growth, and a history of corporate staff being the ones cut when deals slow down. On the plus side, the corporate marketing team hires remote, the CMO has been in place since 2021, and losses are shrinking. The $85k-$100k band sits below the $100k-$110k posted for a Senior Manager role on the same team in June 2026, so Joshua should aim for the top of the band or higher. In interviews, he should ask about team size and reporting line (does the role report to the CMO?), whether remote is permanent, how marketing fared in the December 2023 cuts, and how the team handles requests from about 1,700 brokers. It is worth pursuing with clear eyes, but not a standout employer on the available evidence.
