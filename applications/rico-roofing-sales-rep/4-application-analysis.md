# Application Analysis & Feedback — Rico Roofing, Experienced Roofing Sales Rep

## Job Match: Partial

Two honest halves to this one.

### The gap is real and it is in the job title
The posting says **"Experienced"** and **"one-call close."** You have neither roofing sales nor a documented in-home closing record. They will screen for it. Your resume and cover letter do not claim it — the letter says outright that you have not sold roofs, because a closer respects a straight answer and because they would find out in the first interview anyway.

What you do have that maps: six years selling your own services, in-person work inside homes (model homes with Blandford, realtor property shoots), relationships with home developers and construction companies, a year-plus of 5.0-star reviews, and living in Mesa where the appointments are.

### This is a genuinely better setup than the last roofing job
Worth separating from Discount Pro Home, which is already in your tracker:

| | Discount Pro Home | **Rico Roofing** |
|---|---|---|
| Lead source | You knock doors and cold call | **Company-provided pre-qualified appointments** |
| Location | "On the road" | Mesa — your city |
| Ad tone | Hype, "weed you out" | Plain job description |
| Company named | Only after asking | Named upfront |

The lead-generation difference is the big one. Most commission-only roofing roles fail people because they spend months building a pipeline with no income. Rico hands you booked appointments, so your earnings track your closing rate rather than your ability to survive a cold start. That is a materially different bet.

## Still, be clear-eyed about the pay
"$100,000 – $300,000" is a commission projection, not a salary, and this remains a **100% commission, no base, almost certainly 1099** role. The ad also never says how many appointments per week you get, and that single number determines your income more than anything else. Ask directly:

1. How many pre-qualified appointments per week does a new rep actually receive?
2. What is the average commission per closed job, and what is a typical closing rate?
3. What did the median rep — not the top rep — earn last year?
4. W-2 or 1099? Any draw or ramp support in the first 90 days?
5. Are there chargebacks if a job cancels or financing falls through?

## Verify them first — this takes ten minutes
1. **AZ Registrar of Contractors** — search "Rico Roofing" at roc.az.gov. Confirm an active license and check for complaints. A roofer without a clean ROC record is a hard no.
2. **Google reviews and BBB** — read the *installation* reviews, not just the sales ones. You would be selling that workmanship to your neighbors, in the city where you live.

## ATS / recruiter read
Covered: in-person client presentation, homeowner relationships, construction and residential clients, self-directed, goal tracking, reliable transportation, works outdoors, local.
Not claimed: roofing sales, one-call close record, in-home retail sales.

## Interview prep
- **"Have you sold roofs?"** — No. Then pivot: six years closing your own deals with homeowners and developers, and a track record of doing what I said I would do. Ask what their ramp looks like for a strong closer from an adjacent field.
- **Your Spartan finish and construction-video work** both land well with this audience.
- Ask to **ride along on an appointment** before committing. Same logic as the shadow day — it costs you an afternoon and tells you everything about the pitch, the products, and the customer.

## Bottom line
Worth applying and worth the conversation, with the appointment-volume question answered before you commit to anything. Compared with the other commission roles in your tracker, this is the better structured one. Compared with Valleywise or a salaried marketing role, it is still the higher-risk path — so run it as a parallel option, not a replacement.
