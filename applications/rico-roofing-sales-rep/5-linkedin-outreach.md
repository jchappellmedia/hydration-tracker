# Outreach Strategy — Rico Roofing

Small local roofer in Mesa. The owner or sales manager likely reads applications personally, so a short direct note carries real weight here.

## Who to target
Search LinkedIn for **"Rico Roofing"** and filter by People, or check the company's website for an About/Team page:
1. **Owner / President** — at a company this size, usually the decision-maker.
2. **Sales Manager** — runs the rep team and the appointment pipeline.
3. **A current sales rep** — the honest source on appointment volume and real earnings.

## Messages

**To the owner or sales manager**
> Subject: Roofing Sales Applicant — Mesa
>
> Hi [Name], I applied for your roofing sales position. Straight up: I have not sold roofs. What I have done is run my own business for six years selling face to face — filming model homes for Blandford Homes, working with realtors and developers, and holding 5.0-star reviews for over a year. I live in Mesa and I close because my income has always depended on it. I'd welcome a conversation, and I'd happily ride along on an appointment.

**To a current rep** *(send this one first if you can find someone)*
> Subject: Quick question about the rep role
>
> Hi [Name], I'm applying for a sales role at Rico and would value an honest read from someone doing the job. How many appointments a week do you typically run, and what does a solid month look like? Fifteen minutes would mean a lot.

## Why the ride-along ask matters
Offering to ride along signals you are serious rather than shopping, and it answers the questions that decide whether this job is worth taking — appointment quality, product, pitch, and how homeowners actually respond. It costs you an afternoon and it is the single most informative thing you can do before saying yes.

## Tone
Direct, no corporate polish, no overselling the gap. Owners of small trades businesses respect someone who names what they do not know and shows up anyway.
