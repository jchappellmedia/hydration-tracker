Company research

Zillow Group is a Seattle-based real estate marketplace with about 7,000 employees (7,058 as of March 31, 2026, per Inman). Since 2020 it has run a remote-first "Cloud HQ" model. Jeremy Wacksman is CEO; he succeeded co-founder Rich Barton. The Video Content Producer role appears to sit in StudioZ, Zillow's in-house creative agency. Job-board copies of the posting describe it as building StudioZ's growing video operation, including YouTube publishing, calendars, packaging, performance reporting and testing. They list a remote US base pay range of $89,200 to $150,100 that varies by state. The business is growing (Q2 2026 revenue was $772M, up 18% year over year). Even so, 2026 brought two layoff rounds (about 200 in January, more than 500 in August), a COO exit, and several open lawsuits, including an FTC and state suit over the Redfin deal. Employee ratings have fallen. Older Glassdoor snapshots showed 4.6/5. The 2026 snapshots show about 3.3 to 3.4/5, and only about half of reviewers would recommend the company. All the review data found is company-wide. I found no Glassdoor reviews specific to marketing, creative, StudioZ or video teams. The only department-level signal is a small, undated set of Comparably marketing-team comments.

What employees say: pros and cons

Pros

  • Remote-first by policy, not as an exception. Under the Cloud HQ model about 84% of employees are fully remote with no assigned office, and Zillow cut its office footprint by 73% (11 offices down to 6). Leadership defended remote work in 2023 when Seattle pushed for a return downtown, and again in 2025, citing a large rise in job applicants. This fits a Remote (US) role. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Strong benefits on paper. A 2023 update raised parental leave to 12 weeks at full pay, plus 8 weeks of pregnancy disability leave for birth parents (20 weeks total). It also added fertility coverage through Progyny and 10 days of paid leave after the loss of a child. Glassdoor reviewers often list benefits as a pro. The detailed figures are from 2023; I found no 2026 benefits update. [1] [2]
  • Work-life balance and coworkers are the most-praised items across sites. Indeed reviewers cite balance and communication, Glassdoor reviewers cite supportive colleagues, and work-life balance is Zillow's highest-rated Blind category. [1] [2] [3]
  • The role sits in a team that is investing in video. Postings describe StudioZ as a full-service in-house creative agency (creative, content, strategy, production) with a growing video operation that includes YouTube publishing, packaging, performance reporting and testing, plus a Seattle studio for shoots. [1] [2] [3]
  • The pay range is published and in line with related roles. The Video Content Producer posting lists $89,200 to $150,100 base, set by state. Related StudioZ and brand roles list similar bands (Content Creator $89,200 to $150,100; Creative Producer about $90,900 to $145,100). [1] [2]
  • Revenue is growing even with the cuts. Q1 2026 revenue was $708M and Q2 was $772M, both up 18% year over year. Coverage describes the August layoffs as cost discipline, not a rescue. [1] [2]
  • Outside recognition through 2025. Zillow ranked #43 on Fortune's 100 Best Companies to Work For in 2025. Great Place to Work reported that 93% of employees endorsed the company, against 57% at a typical company. That survey predates the 2026 layoffs, and I could not find a 2026 rank. [1] [2] [3]

Cons

  • Two layoff rounds in 2026. About 200 people were let go in January in what Zillow called performance-related cuts tied to its review cycle. More than 500 (about 7% of staff) were cut in August for cost reasons, and Zillow has not said which teams were hit. This follows larger cuts in past years: about 2,000 when Zillow Offers shut down, and about 300 more later. Glassdoor reviewers cite rolling layoffs and a loss of trust. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
  • Employee sentiment has dropped. Glassdoor went from about 4.6/5 with 92% recommending (early 2025 snapshots) to about 3.3 to 3.4/5 with about 49% to 55% recommending in 2026. CEO approval is about 53% to 58%. Some reviewers link the change to the CEO transition from Barton to Wacksman. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Frequent reorgs and weak middle management. A 2026 Glassdoor review cites quarterly reorgs and priorities that shift every six weeks. Management is Zillow's lowest-rated Blind category (3.1/5), and several Indeed and Glassdoor reviewers say the experience depends on the manager. [1] [2] [3]
  • Pay and raise complaints. Reviewers describe below-market pay, compensation that fell over the past couple of years, and annual increases that do not cover inflation. They also say recognition is lacking. [1] [2] [3]
  • Leadership churn in 2026. COO Jun Choo left in August, and CFO Jeremy Hofmann took on the COO role as well. The chief accounting officer stepped down in September, and a VP of industry engagement with nearly 20 years at Zillow left in August. An earlier shakeup included the departure of president Susan Daimler. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Heavy legal exposure. The FTC and state attorneys general are suing over the Redfin rental-listing deal; the court denied Zillow's motion to dismiss in May 2026. Zillow also faces a Compass antitrust suit, a CoStar copyright suit over rental photos, and a shareholder class action. Zillow has disclosed elevated legal expenses that weigh on margins. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • Remote work can limit visibility and growth. Reviewers say promotions depend on senior leaders seeing your work, which is harder from home, and that career growth is limited. Some remote staff say they miss in-person connection. [1] [2] [3]

Watch for

  • Two layoff rounds in 2026: about 200 in January and more than 500 (about 7%) in August. Zillow has not said which teams were cut, so it is unknown whether StudioZ or brand marketing was affected.
  • The Glassdoor rating fell from about 4.6 (early 2025 snapshots) to about 3.3 to 3.4 in 2026, and only about half of reviewers would recommend the company.
  • C-suite change in August 2026: the COO left and the CFO now holds both roles. The chief accounting officer also stepped down in September 2026.
  • Open legal fights with the FTC and states (motion to dismiss denied May 2026), Compass and CoStar, plus a shareholder class action. Zillow has disclosed elevated legal costs.
  • A 2026 Glassdoor review reports quarterly reorgs and priorities that change every six weeks.
  • The CEO is pushing staff to adopt AI. Zillow has not said whether AI played a role in the August cuts. Ask how AI tools are expected to change video and content production on this team.
  • One recent Glassdoor review, surfaced only in a search summary, says Zillow eliminated a wage-replacement benefit (up to 80 hours of pay during approved leave). A follow-up search did not confirm it, so ask HR rather than relying on it.
  • A related StudioZ posting (Digital Video Writer & Host) requires regular shoots in Zillow's Seattle studio. Confirm whether the Video Content Producer role carries any travel or on-site expectation despite being listed as Remote (US).

Bottom line

Zillow is a reasonable target for a remote video producer. It is committed to remote work, the published pay band is $89,200 to $150,100, benefits look strong on paper, and StudioZ is building out its video and YouTube operation. The risk is stability. Two 2026 layoff rounds, a COO exit, ongoing lawsuits and a Glassdoor drop from about 4.6 to about 3.3-3.4 point to a company cutting costs while it grows revenue. All review data is company-wide, and nothing found speaks to StudioZ or creative teams directly. Questions to ask in interviews: Was StudioZ or brand marketing affected by the August 2026 layoffs, and how large is the team now? Is this a new or backfilled role? Who does the role report to, and how long has that manager been in place? How are video goals and YouTube metrics set, and how often have priorities changed this year? How is the team using AI tools, and what does that mean for this role's scope? Are there travel or Seattle studio expectations? Where in the posted pay band will the offer land for my state, and is equity included? Are the 2023 parental leave and other benefits still current?
