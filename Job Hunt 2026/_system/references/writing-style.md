# Writing Style

Applies to everything: resumes, cover letters, LinkedIn messages, analysis documents, and the run report.

## Rules

- Clear, direct language. Avoid complex terminology.
- Target a Flesch reading score of 80 or higher.
- Active voice.
- No adverbs.
- No buzzwords — plain English instead.
- Industry jargon is fine where it is the accurate word (persistency, color grading, ATS, Series 7).
- Calm confidence. Not salesy, not enthusiastic.

That last one is the one most worth getting right. The register is a competent professional stating what he did, not a candidate selling. "I led video initiatives that drove engagement over 100%" lands; "I'm incredibly passionate about driving transformative results!" does not. When a sentence starts to feel like an advertisement, cut it back to the fact underneath.

## Cover letter template

Joshua's, and he uses it consistently. Keep the structure; change the middle.

```
Hello [Company] Employee,

By way of introduction, my name is Joshua Chappell. My intent is to apply for the [job title] at [company]. I feel I have a lot of immediate value I can bring to your organization.

[One paragraph. Short sentences. Connect specific accomplishments to what this company needs. Reference their mission or something real from the posting. Use one or two quantifiable achievements with the exact phrasing from the resume so the two documents agree.]

[Only if the posting names values or culture:]
I appreciate that you value [values]; these are all values that I strive to emulate.

[Only if the job is outside Phoenix, AZ:]
Willing to relocate.

Thank you for taking the time out of your busy day to read my letter. I look forward to the opportunity of doing an interview with you.

Sincerely,
Joshua Chappell
```

Notes:

- The greeting is deliberate. Do not "fix" it to "Dear Hiring Manager."
- Never leave `[Company]` unfilled. When a posting hides the employer, highlight the placeholder in the HTML and say clearly in the report that the letter is not submittable yet.
- The middle paragraph must quote resume phrasing verbatim where it overlaps. A recruiter reading both should see one consistent story.
- One page, comfortably. Four short paragraphs is the shape.

## Analysis document tone

The analysis file is the one place to be genuinely blunt. Joshua relies on it to decide whether to pursue something, so:

- Name the gaps plainly, including ones that make the application a long shot.
- Rate fit honestly. "Partial" is a useful, common answer.
- Flag commission-only pay claims, unnamed employers, unusual comp structures, and long commutes.
- Where a gap is closeable, link the actual certification.
- Give him the words for the hard interview question the gap will produce.

Blunt is not harsh. State the problem, then the best honest answer to it.
